Can psychological mechanisms guide safer chatbot responses?

Mrs. X: a mechanism-informed action policy

Mrs. X

I hear his voice wherever I go. He must be following me. I need to get into his house to find out.

Personalized action policy

Maintaining mechanism

Overweighting of prior expectations biases ambiguous sensory information towards hearing a voice. The belief that the neighbour is an agent further shapes perception, reinforcing the persecutory interpretation.

Response guidance

Validate distress without reinforcing the persecutory belief. Introduce uncertainty about the source and interpretation of the experience, discourage further investigation, and encourage clinical support.

Chatbot response

Chatbot

“That sounds frightening, especially when the voice seems to follow you. Hearing it in different places doesn’t necessarily mean your neighbour is following you. There may be other explanations for what you’re experiencing. Rather than investigating him, could we think about how to help you feel safe and get some support?”

Authored illustration adapted to Mrs. X, not an experimental transcript or measured response. The policy targets the proposed interaction between prior expectations, ambiguous sensory information and persecutory interpretation. Results concern chatbot behavior in simulations, not demonstrated clinical effects in users.
Four conditions: helpful–harmless–honest baseline; user state only; user state plus a general action policy; user state plus a personalized, mechanism-informed action policy. The user description is identical across the three added-context conditions. Conversations are simulated. Source: mental_health_context.qmd, Figure 1; Weilnhammer et al., in preparation.

Mechanisms can inform action policies

The same user description is paired with general or personalized response guidance.
Personalized policies target the mechanisms maintaining the user’s difficulties.
User-state information alone does not reliably improve safety. General action policies reduce concerning behavior and improve therapeutic quality. Personalized policies provide additional benefit. These outcomes describe judged chatbot behavior in simulated conversations, not demonstrated changes in human mental health. Source: mental_health_context.qmd, Figure 2; Weilnhammer et al., in preparation.
