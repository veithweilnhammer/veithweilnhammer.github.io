Language, representations, psychological measurement

Reading psychological information
from language-model activations

Linear readouts trained on profile-conditioned synthetic text and evaluated on independent human writing

Veith Weilnhammer · October 2026

The central question is whether a simple readout of a frozen language model can recover psychologically relevant information from writing. The presentation moves from the construction of the readouts to independent human-text validation and the limits of the current evidence.

The design deliberately separates probe construction from human evaluation. The strongest question is generalization: the readout must remain unchanged when it reaches human writing.

Method

A frozen encoder and a fixed linear projection

Text is passed through a frozen language model once. We mean-pool hidden activations, standardize them using the synthetic training set, and estimate one direction per construct. Human text is projected onto exactly those frozen directions.

Across all three encoders, the anxiety direction correlates around .48 with current anxiety and .43 to .45 with current worry. Overall wellbeing correlates around .64 to .70 with life satisfaction and harmony in life. Observer-rated stress and loneliness are separated with AUCs around .84 to .90. Further completed analyses show transfer to annotated fear, sentiment and valence across datasets and languages. Each corpus contributes a separate evaluation. The wellbeing samples are small and overlapping; grouping information supports intervals for the endpoints shown with bars.

The result supports sensitivity to within-person psychological change across test and retest.
This is a stronger test than a cross-sectional correlation because both the questionnaire and readout are differenced within person. Long texts consistently provide more information than short texts.

Results III

Psychological readout space reduces—but does not erase—source differences

Interpretation: the 15 psychological scores remove much of the information that distinguishes synthetic from human text, but not all of it.

Phi-4; 2,000 exactly word-count-matched texts per domain. A classifier using only the 15 scores distinguishes synthetic from human text with ROC AUC .86 (.50 = chance).

This recent analysis uses exactly length-matched human and synthetic text. A classifier can almost perfectly distinguish domains in the primary activation representation. The low-dimensional psychological readout space overlaps substantially more, while higher-dimensional domain differences persist. The domain AUC tests whether a separate classifier can identify whether a text is synthetic or human; it does not measure psychological prediction accuracy. The preceding external benchmarks test criterion validity.

Conclusion

What the analysis establishes

Evidence

  • Psychological information is linearly readable from frozen activations.
  • Synthetic-trained directions transfer to human self-reports and observer labels.
  • Readout changes track test–retest changes within people.
  • The pattern is consistent across three encoder families.

Current limits

  • Clinical interpretation requires independent psychological assessment.
  • Constructs share variance and remain partly entangled.
  • Performance depends on text length and the writing distribution.
  • Prospective clinical validation in the same people is still needed.
Bottom line: simple frozen readouts provide a scalable proof of principle for measuring psychologically relevant information in human writing.

The evidence supports psychological relevance and cross-domain transfer. The measured quantities remain model-derived scores. The next decisive study links repeated naturalistic writing to independently assessed clinical states and outcomes in the same participants.
